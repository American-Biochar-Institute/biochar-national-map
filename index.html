Mapped soil conditions may not reflect your field's current drainage, and some mapped soils already represent drained conditions. Artificial drainage removes free water from wet soils with tile, ditches or surface mounding. Choose Yes if the land has working drainage that the mapped soil does not already reflect: the water table and ponding checks are then left out. NRCS itself says it is unknown whether a shallow water table affects how biochar works.

NRCS Soil Taxonomy · Evidence

NRCS assumes biochar raises soil pH, which helps acidic soils most. A biochar's liming value depends on its feedstock and how it was made: many wood biochars have little, while manure-based and ash-rich biochars often have more. Check the calcium carbonate equivalent (CCE) on the product's lab report.

Choose No if your biochar has little liming value. Soil pH is then left out, and the other soil properties share its points, so the score can go up or down. To use your own soil test pH, add it to a field under Your fields.

Evidence

NRCS lowers the rating for steep slopes, flooding and rocky ground, and gives fewer points to shrink-swell clay. These affect how and when you apply biochar. No study validates these NRCS cutoffs, and the few that tested slope found mixed or no effects. Turn this on to leave all four out, or leave them out one at a time. Leaving out shrink-swell can lower a score as well as raise it.

Evidence

Or leave out one at a time:

  • Research summaries found biochar reduced erosion on average. Steep ground is about safe equipment and keeping biochar in place until it is worked in.

  • No study links flooding to weaker biochar benefits. Floodwater can carry loose biochar away, so apply outside the flood season and work it in.

  • No study links rocky ground to weaker biochar benefits. Rock makes spreading harder and leaves less soil to mix into, so adjust the rate.

  • Research points the other way: in shrink-swell clays, biochar often reduces cracking. Results vary, and low rates can make cracking worse. Shrink-swell adds points rather than setting a limit, so leaving it out passes its points to the other soil properties. Soils that scored well on shrink-swell can go down.
